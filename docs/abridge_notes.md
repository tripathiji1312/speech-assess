# Abridge — Research Notes & Learnings

Sources: abridge.com/ai + /ai/publications, whitepapers, arXiv (ACL 2021 SOAP, AAAI 2024
MedAlign, SALT 2023, Feedback-to-Checklists 2025, HDPO 2026), HuggingFace org (checked Aug 2026).

## What they do
Ambient AI clinical scribe: ASR on doctor-patient conversation -> structured note -> guardrails
-> clinician review -> EHR. 150+ health systems, Best in KLAS 2025/2026.
**Critical fact: HuggingFace org has 0 public models, 0 public datasets.** Papers/whitepapers are
open; the models are proprietary. MedAlign is Stanford's dataset (research DUA), not Abridge's.
"Open models available free" is NOT true — the *ideas* are free.

## Paper 1: SOAP Notes via Modular Summarization (ACL 2021)
Spectrum of extract->abstractive decomposition: Conv2Note (end-to-end) < Ext2Note
< Ext2Sec < Cluster2Sent (per-section, per-sentence clusters + evidence linking). More
extractive = better; small T5 models feasible because inputs/outputs are small per step.
Every summary sentence tied to supporting transcript utterances.
**Takeaway:** never summarize the whole conversation at once; section/pass-based generation
with evidence linkage. Validates our pass-1 extraction JSON + short-context design.

## Paper 2: MedAlign (AAAI 2024, Stanford + collaborators)
983 clinician-written EHR instructions; 303 gold answers; 276 EHRs. Clinician evaluation of 6
LLMs: error rates 35% (GPT-4) -> 68% (MPT-7B). **8.3% accuracy drop when context cut
32k -> 2k tokens.** BM25 retrieval matched instructions to relevant EHRs 74% of the time.
Automated NLG metrics correlated with clinician rankings.
**Takeaway:** short context hurts, so choose WHICH context matters (retrieval first);
2k tokens is our budget — retrieve the right 2k. Evaluate with task-specific instructions,
not exam QA.

## Paper 3: The Science of Confabulation Elimination (whitepaper, Aug 2025)
Framework: every claim scored on two axes — Support (supported / unsupported) x Severity.
Pipeline: first-draft note -> **purpose-built small detection model** (trained on 50k+ curated
examples) detects each claim + gives reasoning -> correction system fixes/removes unsupported
claims using transcript + EHR context -> clinician review with "Linked Evidence".
Benchmark: 97% confabulation detection vs 82% GPT-4o (off-the-shelf misses 6x more).
1,000+ hours of physician annotation; 10k+ encounter internal benchmark.
**Takeaway:** a tiny task-specific detector beats a big generalist model; detection +
correction is a distinct model stage, not the generator "being careful"; unsupported-claim
training data = realistic claims + modified unsupported variants; human review mandatory.

## Paper 4: SALT — Sequence Alignment (un)Likelihood Training (2023, UMass + Abridge)
Train on (model_output, human_edited_output) pairs: likelihood on edits + unlikelihood on
model's wrong tokens. Beats plain DPO on edit data. "Imitation edits" = simulate edits using
existing ground truth when no human edits available.
**Takeaway:** when users correct the model, prefer edit-based alignment over DPO with
arbitrary negatives; imitation edits make it cheap.

## Paper 5: From Feedback to Checklists (2025, UChicago + Abridge)
22k real encounters of clinician free-text feedback + star ratings -> LLM distilled ~7.7k
feedback items into a 20-question checklist for note quality. Checklist as LLM-as-judge rubric:
0.70 accuracy predicting star ratings vs 0.62 baseline; robust to perturbations.
**Takeaway:** evaluation rubrics derived from real user feedback beat generic judges.
Build checklists, not vibes, for eval.

## Paper 6: HDPO — Hallucination-Detection-Guided Preference Optimization (2026)
Preference pairs generated by a hallucination detector; DPO on them. Evolution of the SALT
line: detector -> preference data -> alignment.
**Takeaway:** our dpo.jsonl (chosen = grounded, rejected = swapped-topic) is the same idea;
upgrade: score negative severity and weight the loss.

## What we copy (mapped to our pipeline)
1. **Claim-support detector stage** (on-device, tiny model): after extraction, check each
   JSON field against the transcript. Train on procedurally generated unsupported variants
   (swap meds, fake urgency, invented numbers). Cheap: reuse our generators.
2. **Support x Severity taxonomy in eval**: not just "wrong" — is it unsupported? how severe?
   Add to eval_harness.
3. **Evidence linkage**: each extracted field -> transcript span (extend schema later;
   powers user-facing "see source" = Abridge's Linked Evidence).
4. **Checklist-based eval**: build 10-20 question rubrics per task for LLM-judge; derive from
   our seed feedback (or hand-write from failure cases).
5. **Short, retrieved context**: 2k token budget; BM25/embedding retrieval over KB first.
6. **Modular passes**: already in our design (extraction pass + QA pass). Don't merge.
7. **Edit-based alignment**: when real users correct outputs, fine-tune on
   (wrong_output, corrected) pairs with unlikelihood; imitation edits as fallback.
8. **Human-in-the-loop always**: clinician review before anything clinical; no exceptions.

## Reality check for the user
- Their models/datasets are NOT open (0 HF models). Don't plan on downloading an Abridge model.
- Their data (doctor-patient audio + EHR) is what makes the system; we cannot replicate
  with our QA-pair corpus — but our procedural generators + grounding cover the same
  behaviors at smaller scale.
- Abridge validates the small-task-specific-model architecture at massive scale; edge
  deployment of that architecture is the differentiator we're building.
